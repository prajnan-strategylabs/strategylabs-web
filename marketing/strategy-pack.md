## 1. Trend-Following

> **"Buy BTC when the 50-day moving average is above the 200-day moving average. Sell when it crosses back below."**

The classic golden-cross / death-cross trend filter. It tests one question: does riding established uptrends and sitting out downtrends beat just holding? Trend-following usually trades rarely and can lag turns — a good baseline to measure everything else against.

## 2. Mean-Reversion

> **"Buy ETH when the price drops more than 10% below its 20-day average. Sell when it returns to the average."**

The opposite bet from trend-following: that big drops snap back. Tests whether buying fear and selling the recovery actually pays — or whether "cheap" just keeps getting cheaper. Watch the drawdown on this one; mean-reversion can look brilliant until it isn't.

## 3. Breakout

> **"Buy SOL when it closes above its highest price of the last 30 days. Exit if it falls 8% from the entry."**

Donchian-style breakout. The idea: new highs attract momentum, so you join the move as it breaks out and cap your downside with a hard stop. Tests whether chasing strength works, or whether you're just buying the top before the fade.

## 4. RSI

> **"Buy BTC when the 14-day RSI falls below 30. Sell when RSI rises above 70."**

The textbook oversold/overbought oscillator. Tests the timeless claim that markets bounce when they're "oversold" and stall when "overbought." A clean way to see whether one of crypto Twitter's favorite indicators holds up over 8 years instead of one lucky chart.

## 5. Moving-Average Crossover

> **"Buy ETH when the 10-day moving average crosses above the 30-day moving average. Sell when it crosses back below."**

A faster cousin of #1. Shorter averages mean more trades and quicker reactions — but also more whipsaws and fees. Run it next to the 50/200 version to see the real trade-off between being early and being wrong more often.

## 6. Volatility

> **"Only buy BTC when 20-day volatility is below its 6-month average, using a 50-day moving average trend filter. Stay in cash when volatility spikes."**

A volatility regime filter. Tests an underrated idea: that *when* you're in the market matters as much as *what* you buy. Does sitting out the chaotic, high-volatility stretches improve your returns and shrink your drawdown? Often the answer surprises people.
